Title: Sonitra: A Controlled Benchmarking Toolkit for Automatic Music Transcription

Automatic music transcription (AMT)—the process of converting audio recordings into symbolic scores—is difficult to evaluate fairly because most benchmarks rely on a single, fixed recording environment. Consequently, a poor score cannot be attributed to algorithmic weakness rather than difficult recording conditions. ***Sonitra*** addresses this by systematically varying acoustic factors such as reverberation, dynamic-range compression, and signal distortion to measure how these common degradations affect transcription accuracy across a consistent set of reference scores.

The toolkit uses a modular four-stage pipeline: (1) a digital score (e.g., MIDI) is (2) rendered to audio via software instruments, (3) transcribed back into a symbolic score using AMT models, and (4) compared against the original. Each stage is interchangeable, so different AMT models can be benchmarked under identical acoustic conditions.

Evaluation metrics cover note accuracy, timing, and loudness, following established practice in music information retrieval (MIR); a resynthesis metric aligns transcribed notes against the original recording. Sonitra is at an early stage. Open questions include which metrics best correlate with perceived audio quality, and whether condition-controlled rankings agree with results on standard industry datasets.
